# Game review research

Chess Studio v3 already has the main components of a strong browser-based review. Stockfish scans the game and deepens selected positions. Maia estimates human move choices and outcomes. The symbolic analyzer identifies tactical and positional features, and Review can save positions to Practice. The highest-value next step is to make those components answer a question the player can check on the board. **What does my move allow, how does the alternative change it, and which replies make the difference?** Start with three to five critical decisions per game, using the current browser workers and on-device analysis as the default. ## Compare moves at the same position

**Use Stockfish evidence at the move level.** Keep the reverse scan for triage. For a critical decision, shortlist the played move, the engine leaders, and any plausible alternative that could change the lesson. Search them from the same root, with the same mover perspective and recorded engine settings. A restricted MultiPV search gives a shared comparison when the played move falls outside the original list. When the recommendation hinges on a small gap or a late resource, compare additional single-candidate searches under the same policy, and run a fresh unrestricted search to catch a stronger move that the shortlist missed. Equal budgets improve comparability. Search depth and score can still differ because the tree and search order differ. Store those differences as evidence. [Stockfish UCI search commands](https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/stockfish-wiki/UCI-Protocol-and-Stockfish-Commands.html#searchmoves-move1--movei) · [Stockfish.js MultiPV output source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nmrugg/stockfish.js/v19.0.1/src/search.cpp)

Record the search root, game history, candidate, engine build, node budget, nodes reached, MultiPV, hash policy, depth, score type, bound status, and principal variation. Reuse the score and variation from the same observation. Keep unknown values when evidence is missing. Preserve the distinction between an engine-reported exact score at a particular horizon and an exact chess outcome. Track score gaps and root changes across successive budgets to flag unsettled comparisons. Treat those changes as stability signals until a corpus establishes how well they predict later reversals. When translating a child mate score to the parent, account for the extra ply. Compare same-outcome mating lines on speed only when that distinction matters to the lesson. [Current search collector](../engine.js#L121) · [Stockfish.js mate and WDL formatting](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nmrugg/stockfish.js/v19.0.1/src/uci.cpp)

Allocate extra search where it could change a decision or explanation. Useful signals include a late change of best move, a grade near a boundary, a shallow-to-deep reversal, a disputed mate, a credible unseen defense, and high unevaluated Maia probability mass. A game-wide budget and a cap per decision keep cost bounded. A small sample of apparently quiet positions can reveal missed turning points during evaluation. The current first pass has only one variation, so its sharpness cannot yet measure a gap between the top candidates. [Sharpness calculation](../review.js#L122) · [Current triage](../review.js#L918)

Keep the full win, draw, and loss distribution alongside the expected score. A position that is likely drawn and one with equally likely wins and losses can both score 50%, but they teach different decisions. Describe preserved or lost outcomes directly when a tablebase can establish them. For game accuracy, show how many decisions contributed and label the calculation as Chess Studio's. It uses a Lichess-style weighting blend with Stockfish expected scores and project-specific exclusions. Lichess documents its own centipawn conversion and aggregation. [Chess Studio scoring](../review.js#L97) · [Chess Studio aggregation](../review.js#L1064) · [Lichess accuracy methodology](https://lichess.org/page/accuracy)

## Explain the difference on the board

Keep a small tree for each selected moment. Start with the played move and its strongest refutation, the recommended move and its strongest resistance, and one plausible human alternative or reply. Replay every line through `chessops`. Locate the earliest concrete event that explains why the lines diverge. Compare the threatened square, the legal defenders, the exchange sequence, and the resulting board. A motif earns a place in the coach text when it appears on the relevant branch and explains a consequence that matters to the decision. Static exchange evaluation and NNUE are useful for proposing what to inspect. For example, `seeMove` builds an exchange sequence from attack sets, so a pinned recapturer or a forced response to check needs a legal replay before it supports a claim. [Exchange model](../chess-concepts.js#L162) · [Motif extraction](../chess-concepts.js#L512) · [Pinned NNUE trace](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nmrugg/stockfish.js/v19.0.1/src/nnue/nnue_misc.cpp)

- **Tactics.** If a pass search suggests a threat, test the actual threat after both the played move and the proposed defense. The reply may be illegal, legal but harmless, or still decisive. Search a specific acceptance for a purported poisoned sacrifice and another reply that declines it. A line where the engine declines supplies one branch of that explanation.
- **Strategic plans.** Group viable moves around an observable objective such as a pawn break, a blockade, a favorable exchange, or a king route. Verify the prerequisite and the opponent's strongest interference. Track whether the target feature persists through the line or becomes relevant only after a sequence. Present it as a plan the line supports. Player intent requires separate evidence.
- **Review interaction.** Put one or two sentences above the existing variation controls. Link each factual claim to the branch and board step where it becomes visible. A blocked rook check should open the relevant position with the blocking square highlighted. If the analysis supports only a score difference, state the result and show the line without assigning a motif.

Consider the illustrative position `3r2k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/5PPP/3R2K1 w - - 0 1`. White's `1.Rxd8#` wins immediately because the rook covers the back rank and Black's pawns occupy the escape squares. After `1.h3?`, Black can play `1...Rxd1+`. White loses the rook and escapes the check with `2.Kh2`. The comparison teaches when the open file is decisive and when an escape square changes the outcome of a check.

Chess commentary research has used engine representations for comparison and planning. A 2026 preprint evaluates individual chess claims against tools and expert annotations and finds that tool access improves factual correctness while coverage of expert ideas remains limited. Chess Studio can use the same distinction in its own evaluation. A generated rewrite is an optional later presentation layer. A useful test is whether readers learn more from it while every stated move, square, and outcome still traces to the same evidence. [Zang et al., engine-assisted chess commentary](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1597/) · [Hebbar et al., chess commentary evaluation preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04240)

## Model how hard a position is for a human

Maia answers how move choices vary with rating context. The Chessformer paper reports top-move matching of 55.4%, 56.6%, and 57.1% for the 5M, 23M, and 79M Q14models on its benchmark. Those are research checkpoint results on Lichess blitz positions, with time-pressure positions excluded. Calibration for the pinned ONNX export and for games against the app's bots needs its own evaluation. Improve candidate coverage with the 5M model first. A larger model is worth considering if probability quality on relevant positions warrants the browser cost. [Chessformer, sections 4.1–4.4](https://openreview.net/pdf/676f364d7711f25b9eb37a99ef7ce206714883ec.pdf) · [Browser inference worker](../maia-worker.js#L26)

**Measure the probability of finding a good move.** Evaluate the highest-mass Maia moves, always include the played move and the engine recommendation, and spend more search on moves close to the quality threshold. Define a goal appropriate to the position, such as preserving a draw, keeping a win, or losing fewer than a chosen number of expected-score points. Sum the Maia mass of moves that meet the goal. With known risky mass `R` and unresolved mass `U`, the model's risk is bounded by `[R, R + U]` under that classification. For 50% known safe, 10% known risky, and 40% unresolved, report 10–50% risk with 60% coverage. The range describes missing evaluations. The same procedure yields the probability of finding any sound move, which is more useful than the probability of finding one engine favorite when several moves work. Anderson, Kleinberg, and Mullainathan studied a related measure using the fraction of all legal moves that spoil exact tablebase outcomes. Maia adds a model of how humans choose among those moves. [Assessing Human Error Against a Benchmark of Perfection](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/assessing-human-error.pdf) · [Current Maia metrics](../review.js#L556)

**Search likely replies as well as strongest replies.** For each sound candidate, let Stockfish establish its estimated value under best defense. Then evaluate several high-probability replies from Maia at the resulting position. Weight the replies by their model probabilities and carry the unevaluated tail through the estimate. If covered branches contribute `S = Σ p(reply) × value(reply)` on a 0–1 scale and unresolved replies have total mass `U`, the one-reply estimate lies in `[S, S + U]`, conditional on the assigned branch values. Keep Stockfish's estimated best-defense score visible and apply a minimum quality floor before recommending a practical choice. The strongest reply should remain in the tree even when Maia assigns it little probability. This can distinguish a safe simplification, a sound move that creates hard decisions, and a trap that fails to a concrete defense. The Maia value head is a separate outcome forecast. One-reply weighting measures what happens when likely replies meet the chosen continuation evaluator. Publish the two quantities with clear names. [Stockfish WDL model](https://github.com/official-stockfish/WDL_model) · [Maia-3 UCI and value head](https://github.com/CSSLab/maia3) · [Allie research on human-aligned search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03893)

**Calibrate over many games.** The current estimator uses at most 16 decisions and a prior based on the bot setting. Its range is constructed from a grid posterior mean and standard deviation with a floor. It is a model-fit summary. Use a user-selected reference or a rolling posterior from earlier games for the current review. Show the observed move count and the rating pool, and check sensitivity to the opponent-rating assumption. Keep game performance, human-like move choice, and difficulty as distinct quantities. Start personalization with a small estimate of which opportunities the player converts by motif or phase, with shrinkage toward the population until there is sufficient data. Train personal move preferences only after showing a held-out gain. Maia4All gives a research example of learned player embeddings, while the existing archive supports a smaller first step. [Current rating sweep](../review.js#L342) · [Local bot calibration](bot-calibration.md) · [Maia4All](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21488)

## How to tell whether it improves review

Use whole held-out games to compare v3 with the proposed analysis under the same browser node budget. Include ordinary positions as well as known tactics, sacrifices, quiet defense, multiple equivalent moves, winning moves near score saturation, opening transpositions, and endings with draw claims. Use four kinds of evaluation.

1. **Decision quality.** Measure agreement with a deeper reference on the acceptable move set, severity, and selection of important moments. Separately score exact tablebase outcomes and discrepancies after a search upgrade. A larger Stockfish search is an adjudication reference with its own limits. Lichess puzzles supply known motifs and ChessBench supplies per-action engine labels from Stockfish 16. Measure current-browser agreement directly on the chosen positions. Split by game and position family to limit leakage through repeated positions. [Lichess puzzle and evaluation exports](https://database.lichess.org/) · [ChessBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04494)
2. **Explanation quality.** Check whether each claimed move is legal and each named piece, square, and consequence appears on the cited branch. Have human reviewers rate whether the explanation covers the decisive reason. Track false only-move claims, unsupported threats, and corrections per explanation. Count useful explanations alongside abstentions so accuracy cannot rise solely by omitting difficult positions. [ACT-Eval preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04240)
3. **Human-model calibration.** Compare probability bins with actual held-out decision frequencies. Use log loss for move choice and Brier score for a binary event such as missing any safe move. Slice by rating band, phase, and available clock context. Separate Lichess blitz, Chess Studio against Stockfish, and Chess Studio against Maia. Record unevaluated mass alongside risk. [Guo et al., calibration methods](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) · [Chessformer training and evaluation conditions](https://openreview.net/pdf/676f364d7711f25b9eb37a99ef7ce206714883ec.pdf)
4. **Learning and delivery.** During hands-on review, check that the claim opens the right board position, alternative sound moves receive credit, and an unavailable model leaves usable engine review. Compare first-attempt success on unseen same-theme positions with v3 Practice, alongside time to the first usable explanation and how often final grades revise the initial scan on representative devices. A successful replay of the original puzzle measures recall of that item.

## Recommended order

1. **Make the scores and evidence consistent.** Align the graph by position, make fallback and trace capabilities match the pinned build, report coverage, and base key grades and their lines on comparable searches. Use controlled fixtures for partial MultiPV output, reversed colors, missing evidence, checkmate, castling, underpromotion, en passant, and draw history.
2. **Ship a move comparison for a few critical decisions.** Add replayable branches and one supported explanation per moment to the existing Analysis tab. Gate only-move and threat claims on the evidence available. Compare on the current Lite worker at the same node budget.
3. **Add practical difficulty and better Practice.** Evaluate high-mass Maia choices, show how much of the policy was checked, and create exercises that accept all verified answers to a goal. Use the archive to build player-specific lessons after there are enough opportunities to measure.
4. **Choose the next specialized investment from the evidence.** Add local tablebase packs if endgame outcome errors dominate, an opening explorer if users need preparation, or a stronger evaluator if material disagreements persist on the critical positions. Recheck those priorities once the comparison view is in use.

The main open product decision is whether any optional lookup may send a position off the device. The proposed sequence works with local analysis. The relative benefit of deeper search, larger models, and different explanation styles remains a measurement question for the pinned browser builds and the app's own games.
